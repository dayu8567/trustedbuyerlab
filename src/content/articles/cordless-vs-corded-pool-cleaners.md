Cordless robots are convenient because there is no power cable running across the pool deck. Corded robots avoid battery runtime limits and can run from a continuous power source, but they introduce a cable that must be routed and managed. Neither format wins for every pool.

## Choose cordless when the route matters

If you want to place the robot in the pool without running a cable around furniture, planting beds, or a safety fence, a cordless design may make the routine easier. It can also simplify storage and reduce the need for a nearby outdoor outlet.

The tradeoff is the battery. Compare the runtime for the cleaning mode you will use, the charging time, and the amount of cleaning the pool needs between charges. A cordless cleaner is most compelling when its battery budget fits the pool without forcing a complicated schedule.

## Choose corded when continuous power matters

A corded robot can operate without depending on a finite charge and may support longer cleaning sessions. The cable can also be a disadvantage if it tangles, creates a trip hazard, or makes storage more complicated.

Check how the cable is managed in and around the pool. A cable that is difficult to route may erase the practical benefit of continuous power.

## Compare the cleaning system, not only the power source

The power source does not tell you how well either robot cleans walls, follows a path, or handles debris. Compare the fundamentals:

- Supported pool type and surface.
- Floor, wall, or waterline coverage.
- Basket capacity and filtration.
- Navigation around steps and obstacles.
- Runtime or cable length.
- Retrieval, cleaning, and storage effort.

Modes and app features can improve the routine, but they do not compensate for a poor fit with the pool.

## A useful decision framework

Pick cordless when you value an uncluttered cleaning setup and its measured runtime matches your pool. Pick corded when continuous operation matters more than cable management and the swimming pool layout supports safe routing.

Look at the complete weekly routine rather than the first setup. The right system is the one you can retrieve, clean, charge, and store consistently through the season.
