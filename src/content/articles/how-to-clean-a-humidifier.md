A humidifier works with standing water, which makes cleaning part of normal operation. The exact method depends on the model, but the priorities are consistent: keep the tank dry when it is not in use, remove scale without damaging parts, and avoid dispersing contaminants into the room.

## Empty and rinse the tank

Unplug the unit before cleaning. Empty any water left in the tank and rinse it with fresh water. Do not store a humidifier with water sitting in the tank.

Follow the manual's list of approved cleaning agents. Do not mix vinegar, bleach, or commercial descalers unless the instructions explicitly say to. A strong cleaner that damages plastic or seals is worse than a simple routine.

## Remove mineral scale gently

If the manual permits descaling, use the recommended solution and dwell time. A soft brush can reach corners, but abrasive pads can scratch transparent plastic and create places for residue to collect. Rinse thoroughly afterward and let the tank dry.

For evaporative models, check the wick or filter rather than soaking it in a cleaner. A stiff or mineral-loaded wick may need replacement. Reusing a degraded wick can reduce output and create odor.

## Clean the base and mist path

Use a damp cloth on the base, mist outlet, and cap as directed. Never immerse the electrical base. Keep water away from the fan and power connection. Check the manual for removable nozzles or covers that can be cleaned separately.

Let every part dry completely before reassembly. If the humidifier will be stored, dry the tank and any filter according to the manual, then keep the unit in a dry place. A sealed damp tank is a bad storage container.

## What not to add

Do not add essential oils, disinfectants, or inhalants unless the product's manufacturer specifically approves them for that model. A humidifier can distribute more than water. Unapproved additives can irritate lungs or damage materials.

## Seasonal restart

Before the first use of a new season, inspect the cord, tank, seals, and filter. Run the unit on a water-resistant surface and check for leaks. If the controls or power connection are damaged, stop using it and follow the manufacturer's service guidance.

The routine is not elaborate. Emptying, rinsing, drying, and descaling when needed prevents most of the unpleasant surprises associated with humidifiers.
