A pool robot is a tool, not a permanent fixture. Small maintenance habits protect its battery, filtration, and moving parts, and they also make each cleaning run more effective. The exact steps depend on the manufacturer, so treat its manual as the authority. The routine below is a practical checklist to build around it.

## After every run

Remove the filter basket and empty it before debris has time to compact. Rinse the basket according to the manufacturer's instructions, then inspect the intake and brushes for leaves, hair, or grit.

Check the outside of the robot for tangled material. Do not pull debris out of a brush or track with force; use the cleaning method specified in the manual.

## Once a week

Look at the brushes, tracks, and seals for wear. Rinse the robot with clean water if the manufacturer permits it, and avoid spraying water into electrical connections or charging ports.

Review the filter condition. Some filters need more frequent rinsing during pollen or leaf season, while finer media may need a separate cleaning schedule. Keep a spare filter only if the manufacturer offers one and you know it is compatible.

## Battery care

Charge the robot as directed. Avoid leaving it connected indefinitely if the instructions do not recommend that practice, and do not store it in extreme heat or direct sun. A consistent charging routine is easier to maintain than occasional long charging sessions.

If cleaning performance changes, do not assume the battery is the only explanation. A clogged filter, worn brush, or obstructed intake can produce the same symptom.

## Off-season storage

Clean and dry the robot before storing it. Remove the basket, charge the battery to the level recommended by the manufacturer, and place the cleaner somewhere dry with the cable and charger protected.

Store accessories together so brushes and filters are easy to find when the season starts again. Follow any seasonal service guidance in the manual rather than inventing a repair routine.

## A maintenance schedule that stays realistic

The best routine is short enough to repeat. Empty and rinse after each use, inspect weekly, and do a deeper seasonal check before storage. Record any replacement parts you need while you still remember why they matter.

Consistent care helps the cleaner remain part of the pool routine instead of becoming another item that needs an unexpected repair.
