Robot vacuums are exposed to the same dirt they collect. Hair wraps around brushes, dust packs into filters, and sensors accumulate a film that makes navigation less reliable. Maintenance is less about deep cleaning than about restoring the few parts that control airflow and movement.

## After a run: empty and inspect

Empty the bin before hair and debris compact. Look at the brush ends, side brushes, wheels, and the intake path. Remove visible hair with the tool or method the manufacturer recommends. Do not pull a brush out of its bearing unless the manual says it can be removed.

Check the filter and clean it only in the way the manual permits. If a washable filter is rinsed, let it dry completely before reinstalling. Reinstalling a damp filter can reduce airflow and create odors.

## Clean the sensors

Wipe the navigation sensors, camera window, and charging contacts with a soft, dry or lightly dampened cloth as directed. Avoid spraying liquid onto the robot. A dirty camera can make obstacle recognition inconsistent, while dirty charging contacts can leave the robot looping back to the dock.

Look at the bumper and cliff sensors for dust and hair. Keep wheels free of carpet fibers so the robot can climb a modest transition and turn correctly. Do not add lubricant unless the manufacturer calls for a specific product.

## Maintain the dock

Empty a self-emptying dock's bag or canister before it is packed full. Wipe the dock contacts and ramp. Check that the robot sits squarely when it returns. If the dock is in a location where the robot must turn sharply, test the approach after moving furniture.

## Follow consumable guidance

Brush, filter, and battery replacement intervals depend on floor type, pet hair, and usage. Use the manufacturer's condition checks rather than replacing parts on a fixed date. A brush with worn bristles may stop reaching carpet fibers even if it still turns.

## Monthly symptom check

Listen for a new rattle, a slower brush, a shorter run time, or repeated dock failures. Clean the likely causes first, then check the support documentation. Persistent error messages and battery changes should be handled with the manufacturer's diagnostic steps.

The routine should take minutes, not an afternoon. If it becomes complicated, simplify it: fewer scheduled runs in a cluttered room can be better than constant rescue missions.
