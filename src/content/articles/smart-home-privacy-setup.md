Smart-home devices rarely arrive as a system. A doorbell, lock, camera, and hub may come from different companies and update on different schedules. Privacy and security are therefore an ongoing setup task, not a single checkbox in an app.

## Inventory every connected device

List each device, its app, the account that controls it, and whether it needs cloud access. Include devices you received as gifts or installed years ago. If a device is in a shared home, identify who can view recordings and unlock doors.

Keep the list somewhere you can find it. When you move or replace a device, remove it from the account and revoke its access rather than leaving an orphaned entry.

## Secure the accounts

Use a unique password for each account and a password manager. Turn on multi-factor authentication where supported. Do not reuse the password from your email account; an email compromise should not become a smart-lock compromise.

Review third-party integrations. A voice assistant or automation service may have broad access. Remove integrations you no longer use. Check whether the manufacturer supports local control and what data remains in the cloud when the device is offline.

## Separate the network where practical

Many home routers allow a guest network. Putting smart devices on a separate network can reduce exposure to personal computers and phones, though it may also block casting or local discovery features. Confirm what each device needs before changing the network.

Use modern Wi-Fi security and update the router firmware. A doorbell at the edge of coverage may drop offline or produce delayed alerts, so check signal strength before blaming the app.

## Update and monitor

Enable automatic firmware updates where available. If you prefer manual updates, set a recurring reminder and check the manufacturer's support page. Do not install firmware from an unofficial link.

Review camera fields of view, activity zones, notification sharing, and cloud retention. Remove clips that are no longer needed, and check whether emergency footage can be exported if a company changes its plan.

## Prepare for failure

Keep a physical key for a smart lock, know how the doorbell behaves when the internet is down, and test the backup plan before you need it. Battery-powered devices should have a replacement schedule, not a rescue schedule.

## A quarterly check

Once a quarter, update the inventory, review access, install updates, and test the physical backup for each lock. The goal is not to eliminate every connected device. It is to keep the system understandable, supportable, and recoverable.
