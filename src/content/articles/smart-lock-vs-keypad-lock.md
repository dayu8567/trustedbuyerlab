A keypad lock and a smart lock both remove the need to carry a key for every entry, but they do not offer the same level of connectivity. A keypad can be a simpler, local tool. A smart lock adds remote access, schedules, and integrations, along with more setup and account considerations.

## Keypad locks: local access without an app

A standalone keypad lock usually stores codes on the device. It can work without a hub, subscription, or phone app. That simplicity is attractive for a rental, a shed, or a household that wants fewer connected accounts.

The tradeoff is visibility. You may need to manage codes at the lock, and the device may not tell you when someone enters. Battery replacement and code sharing still need a routine. Check whether the keypad has a physical key override and how the manufacturer handles forgotten codes.

## Smart locks: remote control and records

A smart lock can connect through Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, or a hub to provide remote status, temporary access, and event history. Some integrate with doorbell cameras or home automation. That can be useful for deliveries, guests, and service visits.

The dependencies matter. Remote control needs a bridge or Wi-Fi connection. Firmware updates may change the app. Cloud history and integrations may require an account. Confirm that the lock still opens locally if the network is unavailable.

## Check door compatibility first

Measure the door thickness, backset, cross bore, and deadbolt. A smart lock may replace the entire deadbolt or only the interior thumb turn. Rental agreements can restrict permanent modifications, so confirm permission before changing hardware.

The door must close and align cleanly. A lock that binds because the strike plate is misaligned will still be a problem after it becomes smart. Installation instructions often specify a minimum door preparation and weather rating.

## Security and account hygiene

Use a unique account password, enable multi-factor authentication when supported, and review who has access. Remove old codes and shared users promptly. Check whether the manufacturer supports encrypted communication and local control.

Do not choose a connected lock solely because it reports events. Physical security still depends on the door, frame, strike plate, and installation.

## The practical choice

Choose a keypad for simple code-based access with minimal dependencies. Choose a smart lock when remote access, history, and integrations justify the extra setup. In both cases, verify the door compatibility and a reliable way to enter if the battery or network fails.
