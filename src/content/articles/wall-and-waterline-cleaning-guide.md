Floor coverage is easy to understand: debris falls, the robot passes over it, and the filter captures it. Wall and waterline coverage asks more from the machine. It has to navigate a vertical surface, maintain traction, and reach the area where water meets the pool shell.

That extra work can be valuable, but it is not automatically necessary for every pool.

## Floor cleaning handles the heaviest debris

Most visible debris eventually settles on the pool floor. Leaves, grit, and sunscreen residue are common examples. A floor-focused robot can be an efficient choice when the pool has simple surfaces and you are willing to brush the walls as part of a regular routine.

The important question is whether floor-only cleaning fits the maintenance you will realistically do. A machine that covers only part of the job is a good purchase only when the remaining work is easy for you to complete.

## Wall cleaning changes the weekly routine

A wall-capable robot follows the pool surface upward and removes material that would otherwise need brushing. This is particularly relevant when the walls show visible buildup or when reaching around the pool with a brush is difficult.

Wall performance depends on the surface and the robot's traction. Before buying, check the manufacturer's supported pool types and read the setup requirements. A cleaner designed around one wall material may not behave the same way on every surface.

## Waterline cleaning is a separate benefit

The waterline collects oils, fine debris, and a visible ring that can be stubborn. A robot advertised for waterline cleaning should be able to reach and scrub that band, not merely climb partway up the wall.

Waterline coverage is most useful when:

- The ring appears quickly between manual cleanings.
- You want to reduce brushing and scrubbing time.
- The pool has surfaces supported by the cleaner.
- The price difference is reasonable for the time it saves.

## Look beyond the coverage label

Two cleaners can both claim wall or waterline cleaning while using different navigation systems and brush layouts. Look for details about:

- How the robot maps or follows the pool.
- Which surfaces it supports.
- Whether cleaning modes change runtime.
- What maintenance the brushes and tracks require.

## A simple decision rule

Choose floor coverage when sunken debris is the main problem and manual wall brushing is already part of your routine. Choose wall coverage when you want the robot to take over a meaningful share of surface cleaning. Pay for waterline cleaning when that band is a recurring pain point and the machine is rated for your pool.

Coverage should follow the work. More zones are useful only when they remove a task you would otherwise have to do yourself.
